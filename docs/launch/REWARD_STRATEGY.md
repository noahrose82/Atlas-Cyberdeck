# Atlas Cyberdeck — Reward Strategy

## Strategy

The first Atlas campaign should minimize fulfillment risk.

That means:

> **Digital-first rewards. Limited permanent obligations. No unnecessary merchandise.**

---

# Draft Reward Ladder

## Signal Supporter — $5

Purpose:

Allow people to support the project without creating fulfillment complexity.

Possible reward:

- backer updates;
- supporter thank-you.

---

## Atlas Early Backer — $15

Possible reward:

- everything above;
- early-access community role;
- digital backer badge.

---

## Founder Access — $35

Possible reward:

- everything above;
- access to pre-release builds when distribution is ready;
- founder-edition digital badge;
- name in a public supporter page if the backer opts in.

Important:

"Early access" must not promise a specific store or distribution mechanism until that mechanism is confirmed.

---

## Atlas Pro Founding License — $69

**Conditional concept.**

Use only if the Free / Pro model has been finalized before launch.

Could include:

- defined future Pro entitlement;
- founding-backers identity;
- no removal of core runtime safety from Free.

Do not offer vague "all future paid features forever."

---

## Lifetime Founder — $99–$149

**High caution.**

Lifetime access can create long-term financial and support obligations.

Offer only if:

- "lifetime" is defined contractually;
- eligible product scope is clear;
- future services with recurring third-party costs are excluded or modeled;
- account/license infrastructure exists;
- the economics remain viable.

This tier is optional, not required.

---

# Higher Support Tiers

Higher tiers should provide recognition and participation rather than custom engineering obligations.

Possible:

- supporter wall;
- private development update;
- group Q&A;
- launch thank-you;
- limited founding-patron designation.

Avoid:

- custom feature promises;
- custom builds;
- one-off device support;
- security consulting;
- guaranteed personal technical support;
- physical rewards without a fulfillment plan.

---

# Core Safety Principle

The following should not become premium-only protections:

- circuit-breaker behavior;
- Safe Mode;
- controlled recovery;
- package-state integrity protections;
- critical runtime safety controls.

Monetization should expand capability, convenience, workflow, and advanced tooling — not charge users to make the base product safe.
