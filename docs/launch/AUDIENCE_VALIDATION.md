# Atlas Cyberdeck — Audience Validation Plan

**Phase:** Pre-Launch / Kickstarter Preparation  
**Checkpoint:** Audience Validation  
**Working campaign goal:** `$15,000` — internal planning target, not publicly locked

---

# Objective

Determine whether enough people are genuinely interested in Atlas Cyberdeck to support a Kickstarter campaign before the public funding goal is locked.

The goal is not to collect vanity metrics.

The goal is to answer:

> **Can Atlas reach enough people who understand the product, trust the proof, and are willing to act when the campaign launches?**

---

# What We Are Validating

Audience validation has four jobs:

1. **Message validation** — do people immediately understand what Atlas is?
2. **Audience validation** — which groups care most?
3. **Intent validation** — will interested people join a list or follow the Kickstarter pre-launch page?
4. **Launch validation** — can enough likely backers be identified to create meaningful first-day momentum?

---

# Why the 75-Backer Day-One Target Exists

The working campaign goal is:

```text
$15,000
```

If the average pledge is approximately:

```text
$60
```

then total backers required would be roughly:

```text
250
```

A useful internal first-day target is approximately **30% of the campaign goal**:

```text
30% of $15,000 = $4,500
$4,500 / $60 ≈ 75 backers
```

The 30% target is an Atlas planning assumption for momentum modeling, not a Kickstarter requirement.

# Validation Signals

## Strong

- people can explain Atlas back in their own words;
- visitors click through to technical proof;
- email signup rate improves after seeing the real Ubuntu demo;
- Kickstarter follows grow consistently;
- people ask about availability or backing;
- audience members share the project without being prompted;
- developers / students identify real use cases.

## Weak

- high views with almost no signups;
- people think Atlas is only a terminal emulator;
- people believe Android root is required;
- visitors repeatedly ask what the product actually does;
- engagement is mostly from friends rather than target users;
- people like the cyberdeck aesthetic but show no product intent.

---

# Validation Questions

Ask prospective users:

1. What do you think Atlas Cyberdeck does?
2. What part is most useful to you?
3. What would you use it for?
4. What would stop you from installing or backing it?
5. Which Android device do you use?
6. Is "no Android root required" important to you?
7. Which matters more: Atlas Shell, Ubuntu, package tools, safety/recovery, or future remote workflows?
8. Would you join an early-access list?
9. Would you follow a Kickstarter pre-launch page?
10. What price range would make a software reward feel reasonable?

Do not ask "Would you buy this?" as the only validation question. Actions are stronger evidence than compliments.
